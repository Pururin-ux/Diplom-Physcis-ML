\title{
Limitations of Shape-Based Spectral and Transport Objectives in
Square-Lattice Tight-Binding Quantum Dots: A Baseline-First Audit
}

\author{A. A. Alvinsky}
\affiliation{
Faculty of Physics,
Belarusian State University,
4 Nezavisimosti Ave., 220030 Minsk, Belarus
}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We audit several shape-based objectives in a controlled square-lattice
tight-binding model of finite superelliptic quantum dots. The study is not a
new numerical sprint, but a baseline-first synthesis of existing closed-spectrum
calculations, surrogate-model comparisons, inverse-screening tests,
continuum-reference checks, a tight-binding-only finite-size scaling gate, and
an open-transport contact preflight. The model uses superelliptic boundaries,
direct Kwant spectra, and the low-energy convention
$E_{\mathrm{kin},k}=E_k+4$. Across these outputs, attractive objectives are
repeatedly limited by simple or methodological baselines. A small multilayer
perceptron does not establish a robust advantage over a physics-informed Ridge
model under structured validation. Spectral inverse-screening objectives based
on gap ratios and doublet splitting reduce to isotropy, anisotropy, or symmetry
baselines. A finite-difference continuum reference is useful for calibration but
not adequate as a high-precision shape-sensitive reference in the tested
protocol. A tight-binding-only scaling gate remains inconclusive, and the
tested open-dot conductance is more sensitive to contact width than to
superellipse shape. The contribution is therefore methodological: a
reproducible negative audit showing where apparent shape, ML, continuum-residual,
and transport signals become unreliable before they can justify a positive
device-design rule.
\end{abstract}
